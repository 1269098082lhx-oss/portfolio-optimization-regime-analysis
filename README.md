# Walk-Forward Portfolio Optimization and Market Regime Analysis

## Overview

This project investigates whether constrained Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO)
can improve portfolio risk-adjusted performance relative to simple benchmark
strategies.

The analysis combines portfolio optimization, walk-forward out-of-sample
backtesting, robustness testing, VIX-based market regime analysis, and return
attribution.

The portfolio consists of:

- BRK-B — Berkshire Hathaway
- GLDM — SPDR Gold MiniShares Trust
- QQQM — Invesco NASDAQ 100 ETF
- VOO — Vanguard S&P 500 ETF

---

## Research Question

Can a constrained Mean-Variance Optimization strategy improve risk-adjusted
portfolio performance relative to equal-weight and passive equity benchmarks?

More importantly, what drives the portfolio's performance during periods of
high market volatility?

---

## Methodology

The project follows five main stages:

### 1. Portfolio Construction

Historical asset prices are converted into daily returns.

Expected returns, volatility, covariance, and correlations are estimated from
historical data.

Mean-Variance Optimization is then used to construct portfolios that maximize
the Sharpe Ratio.

To reduce extreme portfolio concentration, individual asset weights are
constrained to a maximum of 35%.

### 2. Walk-Forward Backtesting

Instead of optimizing the portfolio using the entire historical dataset, the
strategy uses only information available at each rebalance date.

Portfolio weights estimated from historical data are then applied to future
out-of-sample returns.

This reduces look-ahead bias and provides a more realistic evaluation of the
strategy.

### 3. Robustness Testing

Three estimation windows are compared:

- 1-Year MVO
- 2-Year MVO
- 3-Year MVO

The optimized strategies are also compared with:

- Equal Weight Portfolio
- VOO Benchmark

Portfolio turnover and transaction costs are incorporated into the analysis.

### 4. Market Regime Analysis

The VIX index is used as a proxy for market volatility.

Market conditions are divided into three regimes:

- Low Volatility: VIX < 15
- Normal: 15 ≤ VIX < 25
- High Volatility: VIX ≥ 25

Portfolio and individual asset performance are evaluated separately across
these regimes.

### 5. Return Attribution

Portfolio returns are decomposed into asset-level contributions:

Contribution(i,t) = Weight(i,t) × Return(i,t)

This allows the analysis to identify which assets contribute positively or
negatively to portfolio performance during periods of market stress.

---

## Key Findings

The walk-forward MVO strategies achieved stronger risk-adjusted performance
than the passive VOO benchmark during the tested sample.

Results were broadly consistent across 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year estimation
windows, suggesting that the findings were not driven by a single lookback
parameter.

During high-VIX periods, equity assets experienced substantially higher
volatility and stronger correlations.

GLDM behaved differently from the equity assets and generated positive average
returns during the observed high-VIX periods.

Return attribution shows that GLDM provided a positive contribution during
these periods, partially offsetting negative contributions from equity
positions.

These results illustrate how diversification can improve portfolio resilience
during periods of elevated market volatility.

## Key Concepts

**Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO)**  
Portfolio construction based on expected returns, volatility, and covariance
between assets.

**Sharpe Ratio**  
Measures return earned per unit of portfolio risk.

**Walk-Forward Backtesting**  
A backtesting framework where portfolio decisions are made using only
information that would have been available at that point in time.

**Market Regime**  
A classification of different market environments. In this project, regimes
are defined using VIX levels.

**Return Attribution**  
Decomposes portfolio performance into the contribution generated by each
individual asset.

## Limitations

This project is an educational quantitative research study and should not be
interpreted as investment advice.

Important limitations include:

- Historical performance does not guarantee future performance.
- Mean-Variance Optimization is sensitive to expected-return and covariance
  estimates.
- The investment universe contains only four assets.
- VIX thresholds used to define market regimes are manually specified.
- Results depend on the historical sample available for all selected assets.
- Transaction costs are simplified and do not include all real-world trading
  frictions.
- Market regime classification is descriptive rather than predictive.
